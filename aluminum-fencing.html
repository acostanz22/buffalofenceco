Aluminum Fence Installation in Albuquerque, NM

Aluminum is the open metal fence. It marks the line, keeps dogs and kids from cutting through, and lets the wind and the view pass. It does not need stain, and it is not the same product as a steel or iron fence.

When aluminum is the right call

Choose it for a frontage, a pool barrier you want to see through, or a foothill lot in places like Placitas where a solid wall would block the view and catch the wind. It also fits side yards that need a boundary without the closed-in feel of privacy boards.

It is not a horse fence, and it is not a security wall. If the job needs a heavier steel frame or a solid screen, we will point you to metal fencing or wood fencing instead of stretching aluminum past what it is good at.

How an aluminum fence is installed

  1. Confirm the look

    Height, picket spacing, and color. Black is the common request.

  2. Follow the grade

    Sections step or rack with the slope so the gaps stay even.

  3. Set the posts

    Posts are set for the panel system and for rock or caliche if that is the ground.

  4. Gates and latches

    Pool and yard gates are hung so they self-close and latch when the layout needs that.

What changes the cost

We do not publish a price list. These are the details that move an estimate.

Height and style

Taller ornamental sections and decorative picks cost more than a plain three-rail look.

Gates

A pool gate or a wide opening adds hardware beyond the line itself.

Slope

Stepped sections on a hillside take more layout time than a flat yard.

Digging

Foothill rock and caliche change the post price even when the panel price does not.

Questions before you call

How much does aluminum fencing cost in Albuquerque?

It usually sits above chain link and in a different range from wood, depending on height and gates. The soil and the slope matter as much as the panel. We do not quote a citywide per-foot price over the phone without the line.

Can you use aluminum where we want to keep the view?

Yes. That is one of the better uses for it. Open pickets hold the boundary without building a wall in front of the Sandias or a foothill lot. If you need the neighbor screened out, aluminum is the wrong material.

Do you offer black ornamental aluminum?

Yes. Black open picket is the style we install most. Spacing and height are chosen for the job, including shorter front-yard runs where the city limits a street-side fence. We do not treat aluminum, steel, and iron as one catalog item.

Do I need to paint aluminum in the desert?

No. The finish is the maintenance plan. Rinse off dust if you want it to look sharp. You do not stain it, and you do not treat it like a wrought-iron fence that needs a rust watch. If a section is bent, it is a parts replacement.

Can you install aluminum on a slope?

Yes. The sections have to follow the grade instead of leaving a growing gap under the pickets. On a steep foothill lot we plan the steps before the material is ordered. A flat-yard kit forced onto a hillside is how those gaps show up.
