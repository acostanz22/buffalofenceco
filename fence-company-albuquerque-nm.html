Fence Company in Albuquerque, NM

Albuquerque fence work is a soil and wind problem as much as a style problem. West Mesa lots take more wind, valley yards deal with irrigation, and a lot of the ground is caliche. We build to the lot, then the material.

What fence jobs look like on city lots

Northeast and southeast residential yards are often a backyard privacy line, a short front-yard fence, and one gate that has to clear a mower. The West Side is more exposed. A solid six-foot panel there needs posts sized for wind, not for a sheltered courtyard.

North and South Valley properties inside the city can be larger, with irrigation and softer ground near the river. The same wood privacy fence that fits a subdivision patio is the wrong product if the line has to cross a ditch or hold animals. That work is planned as farm fencing.

Materials that fit these lots

Wood and vinyl privacy fences are the common backyard request. Chain link is the practical yard and dog fence. Aluminum is the open line when you want the boundary without a wall. Picket is what usually belongs in the front, where the city keeps fences low.

Permits and local rules

The City of Albuquerque requires a permit for a new wall or fence. Published guidance routes fences under six feet through the zoning counter and taller fences through Building Safety. Front yards are generally limited to about three feet unless you get a variance, and corner lots plus overlay zones can be tighter. We note height and which yard the fence is in so that step is clear before anyone digs. Confirm the parcel, because a lot that looks like Albuquerque may sit in a village instead.

Questions from Albuquerque

How much does a fence cost on an Albuquerque lot?

Footage, material, gates, and whether we are digging caliche decide it. A flat backyard replacement is simpler than a sloped West Side lot with a new drive gate. We price the address, not a city average.

Can you replace a block wall or an old wood line?

We replace wood, vinyl, chain link, and metal fences, and we can remove an old fence as part of the new job. A masonry wall is a different structure. If you want a fence where a failing wood line stands now, that is a standard replacement.

Do I need a survey before you build?

You need to know where the line is. We do not survey. Build the fence on the line you confirm, set back if the neighbor or the city requires it, and do not guess off an old fence that may have been in the wrong place.
