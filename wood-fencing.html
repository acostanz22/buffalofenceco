Wood fences

Wood Fence Installation in Albuquerque, NM

Wood is still the usual backyard fence on Albuquerque lots. It can screen a patio, keep a dog in, or replace a line that has dried out in the sun. Cedar and treated pine are the two materials we actually price.

When a wood fence is the right call

Most wood jobs are rear-yard privacy on city and subdivision lots, especially where a neighbor’s second story or a bare block wall leaves the yard open. Shadowbox is worth a look when you want a screen that lets some wind through. A solid board fence catches more of the spring wind, so the posts have to be sized for that, not for a calm catalog photo.

Rail and post-and-board layouts fit larger lots in the North Valley, Corrales, and the east mountains, where a full privacy wall would look out of place and cost more than the job needs. Front-yard picket work is a different product. That page is picket fencing.

How a wood fence goes in

  1. Walk the line

    Length, gates, slope, sprinklers, and whatever is already in the ground.

  2. Confirm the wood

    Cedar or treated pine, height, and solid versus shadowbox.

  3. Set the posts

    Depth and footing change when the ground is caliche or rock.

  4. Build and hang

    Rails, boards, and gates, with the bottom board off the soil and the irrigation.

What changes the cost

We do not publish a price list. These are the details that move an estimate.

Length and height

A six-foot privacy run costs more than a short rail line of the same footage because it uses more wood and stronger posts.

Cedar or pine

Treated pine is the common budget choice. Cedar costs more and weathers differently if you leave it unfinished.

Gates and removal

Each walk or drive gate, and hauling off an old fence, is its own line on the estimate.

Soil and wind

Caliche, rock, and a tall solid panel on an exposed west-facing lot add post work.

Questions before you call

How much does a wood fence cost in Albuquerque?

There is no single rate. The quote follows the length, the height, cedar versus treated pine, the gates, and whether the old fence comes out. Caliche, rock, and slope change the post work, which changes the number. We price it from the property, not from a catalog.

Can you build a wood fence that stands up to the wind?

Yes, if the posts and rails match the style. A solid six-foot privacy fence acts like a sail in spring wind, especially on the West Mesa and other open lots. Shadowbox lets more air through. We will say so when a lighter panel, or a different material, is the sturdier choice.

Do you offer cedar and treated pine?

Yes. Those are the two woods we install. Treated pine is the usual availability and budget choice. Cedar is the upgrade when you want that grain and are willing to pay for it. We do not invent a mill brand or a lifetime stain claim.

Do I need to stain a wood fence in this climate?

If you want to keep the original color, yes. Albuquerque sun dries wood out and turns an unfinished board gray. Stain or sealer is maintenance, not something the fence does on its own. If you do not want that upkeep, look at vinyl or aluminum on the same lot.

Can you replace a few boards without rebuilding the line?

Often, yes. If the posts are still tight and the rails are sound, we replace the bad pickets or a single panel. If the posts have heaved, rotted at the ground line, or walked out of plumb, patching the boards does not fix the fence. We will tell you which one it is.

Talk through the line

Call or send the lot details. We will tell you if the job is a repair, a replacement, or a different material than you started with.
