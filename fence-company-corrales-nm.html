Fence Company in Corrales, NM

Corrales lots are often bigger than a city backyard, and plenty of them keep horses or sit on an acequia. A solid patio panel is the wrong default. The fence has to hold what is on the property and leave the ditch workable.

What fence jobs look like in Corrales

Horse and mixed-animal lines are a large share of the work: wood rail, pipe, and horse-safe wire, with a gate that clears a truck or a trailer. Residential privacy still shows up around the house itself, separate from the pasture fence.

Ditch banks are the constraint people forget. A fence that blocks maintenance access along an acequia creates a fight you do not want. Tell us where the ditch runs and we keep the line off that access.

Materials that fit these lots

Farm fencing is the main product: rail, no-climb or woven wire, and pipe. Wood privacy and chain link fit the house yard. Vinyl is less common on the agricultural lots, and we will not wrap a pasture in a backyard privacy panel.

Permits and local rules

The Village of Corrales handles its own rules, separate from Albuquerque and Rio Rancho. Setbacks, ditch easements, and animal fencing are local questions. We build to the line you confirm and the access the ditch needs. Check the village before a long pasture line goes in, especially if the fence is also doing a job along a road.

Questions from Corrales

How much does fencing cost in Corrales?

Pasture footage adds up, so the material choice matters more than it does on a 50-foot backyard. A house-yard privacy screen and a horse fence are quoted as different lines. Gates for trucks move the number again.

Can you fence horses without blocking the acequia?

Yes. That combination is normal here. The animal fence stays on the pasture, and the ditch keeps the access it needs for cleaning and water. We walk both before posts go in.

Do you also fence the house yard separately?

Yes. A lot of Corrales properties want a pasture fence and a shorter residential fence around the living area. They can be different materials. One product rarely does both jobs well.
